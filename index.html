▸ Wallet Shipped 2026 — · React, TypeScript, Supabase A personal finance app for Israeli banking. Scrapes my bank and two card issuers nightly, stores only raw rows, and derives every figure on the client — three years of history, ~1,770 transactions, in Hebrew and right-to-left.

The design rule is that the app stores nothing it computed. The scraper pushes raw rows; categories, event tags and rent schedules live in separate tables owned by the app; every total is derived at read time by pure functions. That split is what stops tonight's scrape from clobbering a decision I made last week.

  • Card bills double-count by construction. The bank shows one lump debit, the issuer shows the itemised purchases. Itemised scraping only became complete in Aug 2025, so 25 earlier months had the bill removed with nothing in its place — ₪187,193 of history invisible. Now each billing month counts its itemised rows plus the shortfall against what was actually billed: exact for all 25 months, and it disables itself as coverage improves.
  • The card silently started billing in dollars. Foreign purchases were being summed into shekel totals. The charged/original ratio recovers the rate the issuer used — 3.5907, against 3.592 derived by hand — and the rescaled cycle reconciles to the bank's own figure within two agorot.
  • Rent is declared, not inferred. The first version matched a payment by its exact amount and spread it forward, which broke silently the moment the rent changed and filed a third of each cheque into months the interface could not open. Now the obligation is a versioned monthly cost and payments are matched to it, so cadence — quarterly, annual, rotating between flatmates — cannot move a monthly figure.
  • An assistant with read-only tools. Eight scoped tools over the ledger; answers come back as typed blocks — a figure, a calculation, a chart — instead of markdown, so a chart carries its query and the client re-runs it locally. Prompt and schema stay server-side.

▸ Weight Shipped 2026 — · React, TypeScript, Supabase Weight, waist, steps, training and macros — built because every calorie calculator asks you to self-select an activity level from a dropdown, which is a guess people get wrong in a consistent direction.
  • Apple Health has no web API. Without shipping a native app, the only route is a Shortcuts automation posting the day's step count to an endpoint. It authenticates with a scoped key whose entire blast radius is one step count for one user — deliberately not a long-lived user token, which would grant read and write over everything.
  • The energy model is decomposed, not declared. Basal metabolism, plus walking measured from real steps, plus training from a stated weekly frequency — kept apart so walking is never counted twice, which is exactly what a single lifestyle multiplier on top of measured steps does.
  • Calibrated against a published equation rather than a fitted constant, after the first version quietly told me a stated 1 kg/week goal would deliver 0.42. The floor causing it is now shown on screen instead of silently clamping.
  • Every date is an Israel-local day. Storing a date-only value as a UTC instant had already put 1,750 rows a day early in the other app, so here the local-day derivation lives in one module and nothing else may compute a day.
No demo account on this one — it holds one real person's body data, so there is nothing to hand out. Happy to walk through it live.
▸ Synthetic speech attribution Published 2022 · IEEE Signal Processing Cup Given a recording, identify which synthetic speech generator produced it — attribution rather than detection. 92.2% accuracy on clean samples, 78.4% on noisy ones. Team SIPL, Technion.

Detection asks whether a voice is fake. Attribution asks which system made it, which is the harder and more useful question — it is what lets you trace a clip back to a tool. Our entry combined signal-processing features with deep networks in an ensemble, and leaned on augmentation for robustness, which is where most of the noisy-set performance came from.

▸ Efficient Vision Transformers Research Technion · PyTorch, Hugging Face How much of a Vision Transformer's cost can be removed before the accuracy goes with it — post-training quantisation and inference-time reduction, measured on ImageNet-1k.

Two strands: quantising the PyTorch ViT, and cutting inference time on the Hugging Face model. Both measured against a hand-prepared ImageNet-1k validation set, because label ordering differs between the official dictionary and the one ViT's authors used — get that wrong and the accuracy you measure is meaningless.
